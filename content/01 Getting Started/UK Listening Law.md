# UK Listening Law

> [!info] Not legal advice
> This is a plain-English summary for orientation. Check Ofcom's current guidance and the Act itself if in doubt.

## The short version
- **Owning and using a receive-only scanner needs no licence.** Receivers that can't transmit are exempt (Wireless Telegraphy Apparatus (Receivers) (Exemption) Regulations 1989).
- **Section 48 of the Wireless Telegraphy Act 2006** makes it an offence, without lawful authority, to use radio apparatus *with intent to obtain information* from a message you're not authorised to receive — and also to **disclose** what was in such a message.
- Ofcom's position is that scanners should be used for **general reception**: transmissions intended for anyone to hear.

## Generally fine
- Licensed broadcast stations
- Amateur (HAM) radio
- CB radio

## Not fine
Monitoring services not intended for general reception — for example police, ambulance, fire, private business radio, mobile and cordless phones — is where the offence lies, even though the scanner can tune to many of these frequencies. Passing on what you heard is a separate offence.

## Grey areas
Air-band (ATC and aircraft) and marine VHF listening are widely done by enthusiasts, but their legal status is debated rather than clearly permitted. Streaming such audio online is treated more strictly.

> [!note] Why this matters for this scanner
> The UBC125XLT's [[Service Search]] includes an *Emergency* bank and its coverage reaches public-service bands. Most UK emergency services now use the encrypted digital **Airwave** network (TETRA), which this analogue scanner can't decode anyway — but the legal point stands regardless of what you can actually hear.
